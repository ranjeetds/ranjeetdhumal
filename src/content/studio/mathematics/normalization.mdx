<Chapter id="intuition" title="Distance in the list's own units" objective="Say what a bar is, before the formula.">

A number by itself does not say whether it is large. Large compared with what? The mean of the list is the comparison, and the spread of the list is the unit.

The figure starts at 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 9. The 4 is the mean, so its bar has no height. The 9 is far above the line. Move any one number and you move the mean and the unit, so the other bars move too. They were never independent facts.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="formula" title="Mean, deviation, z-score" objective="Match the caption to the arithmetic.">

For values <Equation tex="x_1, \ldots, x_n" display={false} />,

<Equation tex="\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i x_i," />

<Equation tex="s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2}," />

<Equation tex="z_i = \frac{x_i - \bar{x}}{s}." />

The square root divides by <Equation tex="n" display={false} />, the count. This is the population deviation of these six numbers. It is not the sample estimate that divides by <Equation tex="n - 1" display={false} />.

The defaults sum to 24, so the mean is 4. The squared gaps sum to 40, and <Equation tex="s = \sqrt{40 / 6} \approx 2.58" display={false} />. The selected 9 has z-score <Equation tex="(9 - 4) / 2.58 \approx 1.94" display={false} />.

If every value equals the mean, <Equation tex="s = 0" display={false} />. Division by zero is avoided: every z-score is reported as 0. There is no distance to express.

</Chapter>
