<Chapter id="intuition" title="What is still undecided" objective="Say what the height of the curve is for.">

A coin is about to be flipped. If you already know it will land heads, the flip tells you nothing. If you think heads and tails are equally likely, the flip tells you as much as a flip can tell you. Entropy is that gap, measured in bits.

This figure is one coin. The slider is your probability of heads. The curve is how many bits the flip still carries.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="formula" title="The formula" objective="Compute the number on the caption.">

Write <Equation tex="p" display={false} /> for the probability of heads. Binary entropy, in bits, is

<Equation tex="H(p) = -p \log_2 p - (1 - p) \log_2 (1 - p)." />

At <Equation tex="p = 0.5" display={false} /> both terms are <Equation tex="-0.5 \log_2 0.5 = 0.5" display={false} />, and <Equation tex="H" display={false} /> is 1. That is the top of the curve.

At <Equation tex="p = 0.2" display={false} /> the same formula gives about 0.72 bits. At <Equation tex="p = 0.8" display={false} /> the two terms have swapped places, so the value is the same. The curve is symmetric around one half.

The logarithm is undefined at 0 and at 1. Those endpoints are defined by continuity to be 0 bits: a certain outcome carries no information. The slider runs from 0.01 to 0.99 so the caption never asks for the log of zero.

</Chapter>
