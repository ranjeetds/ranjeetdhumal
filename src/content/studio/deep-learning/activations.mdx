<Chapter id="intuition" title="The bend, drawn" objective="Say what the curve is doing in a neuron.">

[A neuron](/studio/neuron) computes a weighted sum and then bends it. This page is only the bend.

The horizontal axis is the sum, called <Equation tex="x" display={false} /> here. The curve is <Equation tex="f(x)" display={false} />. The marker is the input you chose. The short line is the tangent: if the sum moved a little, the output would move by the slope times that little step.

A backward pass multiplies by this slope. Where the slope is near zero, a change in the sum barely changes the output, and the gradient that flows back is near zero too.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="three" title="The three functions" objective="Match each button to a formula.">

Sigmoid:

<Equation tex="\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}." />

It sits between 0 and 1. Far to the left it is nearly 0. Far to the right it is nearly 1. Its slope is <Equation tex="\sigma(x)\,(1 - \sigma(x))" display={false} />, which is largest at <Equation tex="x = 0" display={false} /> and shrinks toward both ends.

Tanh:

<Equation tex="\tanh x." />

It sits between −1 and 1, and it is an odd function: the value at <Equation tex="-x" display={false} /> is the negative of the value at <Equation tex="x" display={false} />. Its slope is <Equation tex="1 - \tanh^2 x" display={false} />.

ReLU:

<Equation tex="\operatorname{ReLU}(x) = \max(0, x)." />

Left of zero the output is 0 and the slope is 0. Right of zero the output is <Equation tex="x" display={false} /> and the slope is 1. At exactly zero the slope is not defined. The left-hand slope is 0 and the right-hand slope is 1, so any value between them is a subgradient. This figure draws 0 at that single point, and the caption says so. It is a convention for the drawing, not a fact the kink has a unique slope.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="tangent" title="Reading the tangent" objective="Use the slope the caption prints.">

Slide ReLU from 1 down through 0. The output follows the input while the input is positive, then sticks at 0. The tangent is diagonal on the right and flat on the left.

Slide sigmoid across 0. The marker climbs a steep part of the curve and then flattens. The slope the caption prints gets smaller as you leave 0, even while the output is still changing. Far out, the output has almost stopped moving. A gradient multiplied by that slope almost stops too. That is the saturation people mean when they say a sigmoid unit has stopped learning: the bend is flat, so the backward signal is small.

Tanh does the same around both ends, with room to be negative.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="continue" title="What to open next" objective="Use the slope in a backward pass.">

[Backpropagation](/studio/backpropagation) multiplies by a ReLU slope, which it calls the gate: 1 when the pre-activation is positive, and 0 otherwise, including at exactly zero. Close the gate and the gradients of the first weight and the first bias become 0.

</Chapter>
