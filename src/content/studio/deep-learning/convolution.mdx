<Chapter id="intuition" title="The same small sum, slid across" objective="Say what one output cell is looking at.">

A convolution here is not a diagram of a layer. It is a window and a kernel, both 3 by 3, and one number made from them.

The image is 5 by 5 and fixed. The output is 3 by 3, because a 3 by 3 window only fits in three positions across and three down. Select a cell and the window that produced it is shaded. The rest of the image is not in that sum.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="sum" title="The sum of products" objective="Recompute the top-left cell.">

Number the window rows and columns from 0. The raw sum is

<Equation tex="\sum_{i=0}^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{2} K_{ij}\, I_{r+i,\, c+j}." />

The cell on the figure is that sum times the kernel's scale.

Vertical edge has scale 1 and weights

<Equation tex="\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}." />

The top-left window of the image is

<Equation tex="\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}." />

The products are −1, 0, 2, 0, 0, 0, −2, 0, 0. They sum to −1, which is the cell.

Box blur uses a kernel of ones and a scale of <Equation tex="1/9" display={false} />. The same window sums to 7, and the cell is <Equation tex="7/9 \approx 0.78" display={false} />. Sharpen keeps the scale at 1 and puts a 5 in the center, so the center pixel dominates and the neighbours are subtracted.

Nothing here is learned. The three kernels are typed in.

</Chapter>
