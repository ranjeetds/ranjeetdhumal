<Chapter id="intuition" title="Arithmetic, then a bend" objective="Name the two stages before any symbol.">

A neuron here is not a picture of a cell. It is two steps.

First it takes a weighted sum of its inputs and adds a bias. That is a straight line, or a plane, through the inputs. Second it bends that sum with an activation. The bend is what lets a stack of these units draw something other than a straight cut.

This page has two inputs so the sum fits on one line. A layer in a network is many of these units, each with its own weights. You are looking at one.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="sum" title="The weighted sum" objective="Compute the pre-activation from the sliders.">

Write the inputs <Equation tex="x_1, x_2" display={false} />, the weights <Equation tex="w_1, w_2" display={false} />, and the bias <Equation tex="b" display={false} />. The pre-activation is

<Equation tex="z = w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + b." />

The defaults are <Equation tex="x_1 = 1" display={false} />, <Equation tex="x_2 = 0.5" display={false} />, <Equation tex="w_1 = 0.4" display={false} />, <Equation tex="w_2 = -0.2" display={false} />, and <Equation tex="b = 0.1" display={false} />. Then

<Equation tex="z = 0.4 \times 1 + (-0.2) \times 0.5 + 0.1 = 0.40." />

That is the “weighted sum” line. A weight scales its own input. The bias shifts the sum no matter what the inputs are. Set both weights to 0 and the sum equals the bias.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="bend" title="The activation" objective="See what the bend does to a negative sum.">

The output is <Equation tex="y = f(z)" display={false} />, where <Equation tex="f" display={false} /> is the activation you pressed.

ReLU is <Equation tex="\max(0, z)" display={false} />. At the default, <Equation tex="z" display={false} /> is positive, so the output equals the sum, 0.40. Push the sum below zero and the output becomes 0. The negative sum is still printed. ReLU threw it away.

Sigmoid squashes any sum into <Equation tex="(0, 1)" display={false} />. Tanh squashes it into <Equation tex="(-1, 1)" display={false} />. Switch between them on a negative sum. ReLU says 0. The other two still move.

The curves, and the slope of each curve, are the [activations](/studio/activations) figure. This page only applies the function.

</Chapter>
