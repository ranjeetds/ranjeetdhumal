<Chapter id="intuition" title="Four ways to be right or wrong" objective="Name the cells before the rates.">

A classifier says yes or no. The truth is yes or no. That is four counts, not one score.

A true positive was yes, and the classifier said yes. A false positive was no, and it said yes. A false negative was yes, and it said no. A true negative was no, and it said no.

Accuracy adds the two diagonal cells and divides by everything. It can look fine while one of the off-diagonal cells is the whole story. Precision and recall refuse to mix those cells.

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="rates" title="The three rates" objective="See which cells each rate is allowed to see.">

Write <Equation tex="TP, FP, FN, TN" display={false} /> for the four counts.

<Equation tex="\mathrm{precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}, \qquad \mathrm{recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}." />

<Equation tex="\mathrm{accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + FP + FN + TN}." />

The defaults are 8, 2, 3, and 7. Precision is <Equation tex="8 / 10 = 0.80" display={false} />. Recall is <Equation tex="8 / 11 \approx 0.73" display={false} />. Accuracy is <Equation tex="15 / 20 = 0.75" display={false} />.

F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall,

<Equation tex="F_1 = \frac{2 \cdot \mathrm{precision} \cdot \mathrm{recall}}{\mathrm{precision} + \mathrm{recall}}." />

On the defaults that is about 0.76. It is a compromise, not a fifth fact about the counts.

Raise the false negatives. Recall falls, because those misses are in its denominator. Precision does not move, because a false negative was never predicted positive. Set both predicted-positive counts to 0. There is no precision to compute, and the figure says undefined rather than 0.

</Chapter>
