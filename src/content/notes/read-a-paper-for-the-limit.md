Read a paper for the strongest sentence the comparison can support, and stop there. The abstract is allowed to be hopeful. The table is not. When they disagree, the table wins, and the gap between them is the first thing worth writing down. [Separate the claim from the evidence](/knowledge/separate-the-claim-from-the-evidence) is the procedure. This note is about where to look so the procedure has something to bite on.

Look at what was held fixed and what was not. Look at the data the metric was computed on, and at the data the authors say they did not try. Look at a negative comparison if one is reported, and at the place where a natural comparison is missing. A missing comparison is not a scandal by itself. It is a boundary. Inside the boundary the paper can be solid. The mistake is to quote it past the boundary because the abstract did.

## What you then get to keep

You get to keep the method, the measurement, and a sentence you can defend. You do not get to keep a universal. "This architecture can do this task under this budget" is often the real result, and it is worth more than a slogan you will have to walk back. The [research log](/research) on this site is a set of readings in that spirit: a source, the claim it can carry, and the limit.

## What this does not say

Reading for the limit is not a pose of skepticism, and it is not a summary. A summary repeats the abstract in shorter words. A limit tells the next experiment what is still open. If you leave with only the slogan, you have not read the paper yet.
