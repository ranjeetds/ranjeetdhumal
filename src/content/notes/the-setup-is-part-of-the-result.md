A reported score was produced by a setup. The data and the split. The budget, in steps or in data seen. The model size. The decode policy. The tools and documents the system was allowed to read. The metric. Change any of these and the score is no longer evidence about the new setup. It is a story you are hoping will carry.

This is why two papers can quote different numbers for "the same" method. The method's name travelled. The setup did not. [Reproducibility is a procedure](/knowledge/reproducibility-is-a-procedure) is how you keep the setup attached to the number. [Quantization changes the weights you store](/knowledge/quantization-changes-the-weights-you-store) is a small version: a coarser copy is a different system until you measure it. The principle is not special to weights.

## What to put in the first paragraph

Put the setup in the same place as the number, not in a supplement the citation will not mention. A reader who remembers one figure should remember what it was a figure about. [What a table is allowed to say](/knowledge/what-a-table-is-allowed-to-say) fails in a predictable way when the setup is elsewhere: the figure walks off, and the conditions stay behind.

## What this does not say

Listing the setup does not shrink your work. It makes the work usable. Someone who wants to try the method on a new setup knows they are starting an experiment, not inheriting a guarantee. That is the accurate invitation.
